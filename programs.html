Ten ways we remove barriers every day

Each programme is designed with persons with disabilities, their families and the wider Kalobeyei community.

01

Community-Based Rehabilitation

Home and community rehabilitation that keeps therapy close to families.

Trained CBR workers visit households and community points to deliver mobility support, therapy exercises, referrals and follow-up so that rehabilitation continues where people live.

Objectives

  • Bring rehabilitation services within reach of every household
  • Reduce missed follow-ups and preventable secondary conditions
  • Build caregiver confidence in daily therapy routines

Key activities

  • Household visits and functional assessments
  • Guided therapy and mobility exercises
  • Referral and follow-up tracking with health partners

Expected community impact

More persons with disabilities regain daily independence without travelling long distances for care.
02

Early Identification & Intervention

Screening young children so support starts at the right time.

We screen infants and young children for developmental delays, sensory and physical impairments, and connect families to timely clinical and therapeutic intervention.

Objectives

  • Detect developmental delays in the first years of life
  • Shorten the time between identification and treatment
  • Equip parents with early stimulation skills

Key activities

  • Community and clinic-based screening days
  • Developmental milestone monitoring
  • Referral to specialist assessment and therapy

Expected community impact

Children start support early, improving long-term development, school readiness and family wellbeing.
03

Inclusive Education Support

Helping learners with disabilities enrol, stay and succeed in school.

Enrolment drives, learning materials, individualised education support plans and accessibility improvements in partner schools across Turkana West.

Objectives

  • Increase enrolment of learners with disabilities
  • Reduce dropout through tailored learning support
  • Make school environments physically accessible

Key activities

  • Door-to-door enrolment campaigns
  • Assistive learning materials and individual support plans
  • Accessibility upgrades in partner schools

Expected community impact

More learners with disabilities complete each school year alongside their peers.
04

Teacher Capacity Building

Practical training in inclusive and differentiated teaching.

Workshops and classroom coaching that equip teachers with inclusive pedagogy, sign language basics, assessment adaptation and positive behaviour support.

Objectives

  • Strengthen inclusive teaching practice in every partner school
  • Improve communication with deaf and non-verbal learners
  • Embed fair, adapted assessment methods

Key activities

  • Inclusive pedagogy workshops
  • In-classroom coaching and mentoring
  • Sign language and behaviour support training

Expected community impact

Classrooms become places where every learner can participate and be assessed fairly.
05

Assistive Devices Distribution

Wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids and mobility aids.

Needs assessment, fitting and maintenance of assistive devices, plus training for users and caregivers on safe and confident use.

Objectives

  • Match every user with a correctly fitted device
  • Keep devices in safe working condition
  • Build user confidence and independence

Key activities

  • Individual needs assessment and fitting
  • Device distribution and repair clinics
  • User and caregiver training

Expected community impact

Mobility, hearing and communication barriers drop sharply for device recipients.
06

Economic Empowerment

Income opportunities for persons with disabilities and caregivers.

Business skills, savings groups, start-up support and market linkages designed around each participant's abilities and ambitions.

Objectives

  • Raise household income for families affected by disability
  • Grow savings and financial resilience
  • Open fair market access for disabled entrepreneurs

Key activities

  • Business and financial literacy training
  • Savings and lending groups
  • Start-up grants and market linkage support

Expected community impact

Families move from dependency toward steady, self-generated income.
07

Disability Rights Advocacy

Campaigning for accessible services and equal treatment.

Community awareness sessions, engagement with local authorities and service providers, and support for self-advocacy by persons with disabilities.

Objectives

  • Reduce stigma and harmful attitudes in the community
  • Hold service providers to accessibility commitments
  • Grow confident self-advocates

Key activities

  • Community awareness and radio sessions
  • Dialogue forums with authorities and agencies
  • Self-advocacy coaching for persons with disabilities

Expected community impact

Rights are better understood, claimed and respected across Kalobeyei.
08

Parent Support Groups

Safe spaces where caregivers learn and support each other.

Regular peer groups offering psychosocial support, caregiving skills, rights information and collective problem solving.

Objectives

  • Reduce caregiver isolation and stress
  • Share practical caregiving knowledge
  • Enable collective action on shared barriers

Key activities

  • Monthly peer support meetings
  • Psychosocial support and counselling referrals
  • Rights and entitlements information sessions

Expected community impact

Caregivers report lower stress and stronger, better-informed family support.
09

Livelihood Training

Vocational skills matched to local market demand.

Tailoring, agriculture, soap making, catering and other practical trades delivered in accessible training spaces.

Objectives

  • Equip participants with market-relevant trades
  • Ensure training spaces are fully accessible
  • Link graduates to work or self-employment

Key activities

  • Hands-on vocational courses
  • Toolkits and starter equipment
  • Post-training mentorship and job linkage

Expected community impact

Graduates earn from skills that local markets actually need.
10

Partner Linkages

Connecting families to the services they are entitled to.

Referral pathways to health facilities, humanitarian agencies, government departments and specialist providers.

Objectives

  • Close the gap between families and available services
  • Avoid duplication between agencies
  • Track referrals through to completion

Key activities

  • Service mapping and referral pathways
  • Coordination meetings with partners
  • Case follow-up until services are received

Expected community impact

Families reach the right service faster, with fewer repeated journeys and dead ends.
